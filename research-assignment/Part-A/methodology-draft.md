## Part 1: City & Segment Selection

### Why Hyderabad?

**Manufacturing Ecosystem:**
- **Genome Valley**: Concentration of biotech companies (>100+ biotech companies)
- **IT & Electronics**: Strong defence electronics and tech manufacturing base
- **Pharma Cluster**: Established pharmaceutical manufacturing presence
- **Government Support**: Telangana actively promotes biotech and manufacturing

**Data Verification Advantage:**
- Industrial areas well-documented: Hitech City, Genome Valley, Miyapur
- MCA and GST data available and reliable
- Press coverage for major expansions/certifications
- Strong LinkedIn presence of technical founders

**Sector Characteristics:**
- High concentration of specialty biotech (not generic pharma)
- Growing diagnostics segment (post-COVID)
- Emerging specialty chemicals cluster
- Export-focused (WHO/USFDA approvals present)

---

### Why Basket A?

**Segment Profile:**
1. **Specialty Biotech** (probiotics, enzymes, recombinant proteins)
   - Growing market demand in India and globally
   - High technical barriers (differentiation signal)
   - Often founder/scientist-led (technical DM requirement)
   - Active PLI scheme support

2. **Specialty Diagnostics** (diagnostic kits, reagents, life-science tools)
   - Booming post-COVID in India
   - Export markets (WHO, international)
   - Technical depth required
   - Regulatory approvals (USFDA) common

3. **Custom Synthesis & Specialty Chemicals** (pharma intermediates, electronic chemicals)
   - China+1 tailwind (offshore from China)
   - Technical complexity
   - Often small-scale specialized producers
   - Founder-led with science background

4. **Performance Chemicals** (polymer additives, specialty surfactants)
   - Niche markets
   - Export-focused
   - Technical IP-based
   - Smaller revenue but high margins

**Why Strong Fit:**
- All require in-house manufacturing (C1 test)
- Technical expertise essential (C4 test)
- High regulatory/IP barriers (C3 test)
- Growing sectors with tailwinds (C5 test)
- Often promoter-driven startups/scale-ups (C2 test)

## Part 2: Research Sources & Access Method

### Source 1: DSIR-Recognized R&D Labs
**Access:** dsir.gov.in public database  
**Filter Applied:** Hyderabad + Basket A NIC codes  
**Expected Yield:** 80-100 companies  
**Advantage:** Pre-filters for technical seriousness (DSIR recognition = mandatory R&D) 
**Limitation:** Skews toward established, older companies; misses new startups

### Source 2: Industry Associations
**Associations Targeted:**
- ABLE (Association of Biotech Led Enterprises) - biotech members
- BDMA (Bulk Drugs Manufacturers Assoc) - specialty chemicals
- Hyderabad Pharma Cluster - member directory
- Genome Valley Biotech Park - tenant companies

**Expected Yield:** 40-60 companies  
**Advantage:** Members are active, operating companies  
**Limitation:** Only captures association members; misses independents

### Source 3: Manual Google Research
**Search Terms Used:**
- "Specialty biotech Hyderabad" + "recombinant protein"
- "Probiotics manufacturer India Hyderabad"
- "Diagnostic kits Hyderabad India"
- "Specialty chemicals Hyderabad manufacturer"
- "Enzyme production Hyderabad"
- "Fermentation services India"

**Expected Yield:** 30-50 companies  
**Advantage:** Captures newer startups and less-known companies  
**Limitation:** High noise, many irrelevant results

### Source 4: LinkedIn Founder Research
**Approach:** Identify founders with technical backgrounds (PhD/IIT/ISRO), then verify company  
**Filters:** Hyderabad, biotech/pharma/chemicals, founder/MD titles  
**Expected Yield:** 20-30 companies (may overlap with other sources)  
**Advantage:** Strong C4 signal (technical founder found directly)  
**Limitation:** Self-reported data, company headcount often wrong for MSMEs

### Source 5: BSE/NSE Listed Companies
**Filter:** SME platform companies in biotech/chemicals/diagnostics  
**Expected Yield:** 10-15 companies  
**Advantage:** Public financials, verified revenue, leadership bios  
**Limitation:** Only listed companies (smaller universe)

## Part 3: Screening & Filtering Strategy

### Phase 1: Build Raw Universe (Days 1-2)
**Input:** ~120 companies with minimal data  
**Process:**
- Collect: Company name, website (if available), rough segment, source
- Deduplicate across sources
- Note any obvious red flags (e.g., "Trading" in name)

**Output:** Master list of ~100-120 unique companies

### Phase 2: Website Investigation (Days 2-3)
**Input:** 100-120 companies  
**For Each Company, I will:**
1. Visit homepage + About Us page
2. Check Products/Services section
3. Look at Team/Leadership bios
4. Search for facility information
5. Check News/Press/Blog (if present)
6. Note website copyright year (staleness indicator)
7. Look for certifications (USFDA, DSIR, ISO, EU-GMP)

**Quick Disqualifiers at This Stage:**
- "We distribute X products" (trader, not manufacturer)
- "We provide testing services" (CRO/service company)
- Website last updated 2020 or earlier (stale)
- No products section or vague product descriptions

**Output:** ~50-70 companies passing basic checks

### Phase 3: Detailed Scoring (Day 3-4)
**Input:** 50-70 qualified companies  
**Process:** Score each on 6 criteria

**Scoring Methodology:**
- **Strong (S):** Clear evidence, well-documented, verifiable
- **Moderate (M):** Some evidence, reasonable inference, partial verification  
- **Weak (W):** Limited evidence, difficult to verify

**Evidence Collected Per Criterion:**

| Criterion | What I Look For | Evidence Source |
|-----------|-----------------|------------------|
| C1: Manufacturer | Facility mention, production process, equipment | Website, LinkedIn, press releases |
| C2: India-based | HQ location, operational facility in Hyderabad | Company registration, website, press |
| C3: Differentiated | Patents, R&D center, regulatory approvals, niche focus | DSIR list, company website, certifications |
| C4: Technical DM | PhD, IIT/NIT/BITS/IISc, ex-ISRO/DRDO, engineering | LinkedIn founder profile, company bio |
| C5: Tailwind | PLI scheme, Make-in-India, China+1, export focus | Government announcements, company website, press |
| C6: Growth | Recent hiring, new facilities, certifications, press | LinkedIn posts, company news, website updates |

**Output:** ~30-40 companies with C1-C6 scores

### Phase 4: Deep Verification (Day 4)
**Input:** 30-40 scored companies  
**Process:**
1. Final check: Auto-disqualify criteria (PE-owned, subsidiary, stale, etc.)
2. Verify revenue band if possible
3. Write personalization hook (specific recent detail)
4. Make final verdict: Strong Fit / Fit / Borderline

**Output:** Top 25 companies with full evidence

## Part 4: Auto-Disqualify Criteria

✗ **Trader/Distributor** — "We source and distribute" (not manufacturing)  
✗ **Service Company** — CRO, testing lab, analytical services (no products)  
✗ **Generic Pharma** — Bulk APIs, branded generics, vaccines  
✗ **Subsidiary** — Part of large group (Tata, Reliance, etc.)  
✗ **Recently Acquired** — PE-controlled or acquired (not promoter-driven)  
✗ **Revenue > Rs.500Cr** — Outside target band  
✗ **Stale Website** — No updates since 2020 or earlier  
✗ **No Activity** — Last press release 5+ years ago, no hiring signals  
✗ **No Website** — Or single-page placeholder site

## Part 5: Personalization Hook Strategy

Each company will have one "personalization hook" — a specific, recent detail I can use in outreach email line 1.

**Examples of Good Hooks:**
- "First Indian manufacturer of [specific product] with [certification]"
- "Recently expanded capacity for [specific product] to handle [market]"
- "Just hired Director of [role] to scale [capability]"
- "Published research on [topic] at [conference]" in 2024-2025

**Examples of Bad Hooks (generic):**
- "Biotech company in Hyderabad"
- "Manufacturer of specialty chemicals"
- "Strong growth trajectory" (too vague)
